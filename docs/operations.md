# Proposed operation and deployment boundary

This is a design proposal, not an operating utility installation or stakeholder-approved model.

A cooperative/community operator or energy-service provider owns shared assets. Residents choose whether EV charging and tank replenishment can be scheduled. Opt-out restores fixed immediate requests in the model and does not lower criticality. Essential clinic circuits, basic lighting and refrigeration are defined by service need, not ability to pay. Tenants and households without owned equipment are included in the proposed shared-access model.

The operator checks the dashboard and outstanding service obligations daily, explains deferrals and investigates missed deadlines. Complaints go to the operator with an interval/task record, then to the cooperative committee if unresolved. Never promise service beyond available stored energy. Normal demand is proportionally shared in this model; real customer switching and enforcement require a separate detailed design.

Proposed maintenance: daily alarm/availability review; monthly meter, communications and software-backup checks; quarterly trained-contractor inspection of isolation/protection and battery condition; manufacturer-specified battery/inverter maintenance and replacement; authorized recycling at end of life. These intervals are placeholders to be replaced with equipment manuals. Qualified personnel handle electrical faults, thermal alarms and commissioning; local staff do not bypass interlocks.

Required physical dependencies: common connection point, island-capable inverter, transfer/isolation equipment, anti-islanding/protection design, compatible metering, electrical permits/approvals and DISCOM coordination. No instruction in this repository authorizes connection to mains equipment. During public feeder disconnection, unrelated homes are not automatically supplied by a shared battery.

The future DISCOM interface would expose authorized aggregate shortfall estimates and requested/delivered demand response. Individual household telemetry need not be shared. The current simulator has no personal data or external meter connection. Proposed pilot retention: raw operational telemetry 30 days, de-identified aggregate reports 12 months, consent history while participation continues; obtain explicit agreement and applicable review before use. Exports stay local until the user chooses to share them.

No interviews or partnerships have occurred as part of this implementation. Suggested validation questions: which circuits are essential; acceptable EV completion windows; tank buffer requirements; cost-sharing limits; opt-out expectations; who answers after-hours faults; and whether the modeled electrical boundary matches an actual site. External contact requires user authorization.
